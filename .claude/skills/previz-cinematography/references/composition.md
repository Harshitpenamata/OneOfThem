# Composition

Distilled from StudioBinder's guide to composition in art and film. These are paraphrased notes.

- **Rule of thirds** – a 3×3 grid. Put key elements (eyes) on the intersections and the horizon on the upper or lower line. Off-centre placement lets the subject relate to the space around it.
- **Lead room and look room** – leave space on the side a character faces or moves toward. Cut it off to create pressure.
- **Headroom** – eyes on the upper third; trim headroom as shots get tighter.
- **Symmetrical balance** – equal weight on either side of the centre line. Formal, stable, iconic; it can feel uncanny (Wes Anderson). Good for the crash-site reset master and the Old Man as an unchanging presence.
- **Asymmetrical balance** – unequal elements balanced by placement, value, colour or texture. Variety, movement, intrigue.
- **Positive space** – the subject and areas of interest that pull the eye.
- **Negative space** – empty area around the subject. Psychological weight: isolation, loneliness, threat. Frame from further away to create it.
- **Fill the frame** – remove negative space for intensity and detail (CUs, inserts).
- **Contrast** – juxtapose light and dark, colour, size or ideas. The strongest engagement tool.
- **Scale and proportion** – size relative to the human body (scale) and between elements within the frame (proportion). Use them to dwarf or empower.
- **Foreground elements** – something between the camera and the subject (a shoulder, wreck metal, a branch, rain) adds depth and context. Split the frame into foreground, midground and background.
- **Radial balance** – elements radiating from a centre create a focal point and movement (headlight beams, an orbit move).
- **Simplicity** – keep only what's necessary. That doesn't mean simplistic; meaning can still be complex.
- **Golden ratio** – a 1:1.618 spiral placement; a softer alternative to thirds.

Previz checks per frame: Where does the eye go first? Is it the right thing? Is the horizon level (unless it's a Dutch angle)? Is there a foreground layer? Is the negative space intentional?
