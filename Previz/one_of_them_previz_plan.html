Approach

The film is mostly one location played four times. Passes 1–4 at the crash site run from 3:00 to 13:45, which is 645 of the 900 seconds. Every pass opens on the same reset master, cuts to the same radio insert and frozen-rain wide, and ends on black. So the build order follows that: the crash site goes in first and gets locked, pass 1 is animated and shot, then passes 2–4 reuse its marks, set and matched cameras. The opening car scenes, the run and the return come after.

Sequencer is set up the same way as the shot list. One master sequence holds seven sequence-level sub-sequences that match the script's timestamps exactly. Each of those holds a shot track with one shot asset per row of the spreadsheet. Character animation lives in one scene-level layer per scene, so a dialogue scene is performed once and covered by many cameras, the way it would be on set. Shots then only carry their camera and anything specific to that shot, such as inserts.

The first deliverable is a timed skeleton: a 15:00 master of placeholder cameras, in which every shot already has its correct name, duration and lens. It can be watched before any character moves, which confirms the pacing against the shot list straight away.

The continuous orbit, S07-28A/B

The shot list counts one unbroken orbit as two rows that sit across a scene change. It's built as a single 17-second shot asset, OOT_S07-28_Orbit. SQ06 plays the first 12 s as section S07-28A, and SQ07 plays the last 5 s of the same asset as S07-28B. The camera move is one move, so it can't drift between the two halves.

Camera standards

Every shot gets a Cine Camera with a default lens picked from its shot type in the spreadsheet. The spreadsheet's camera-angle column sets the height and position. Unless you give me your real camera and lens set, I'll assume Super 35 at 2.39:1 and a standard prime set.

  • Approve the wreck orientation proposal below.
  • Optional: scratch dialogue reads and a stand-in radio song. Real reads make the long dialogue passes time out properly.

Who: you · Result: unblocks everything after

1Skeleton

Timed Sequencer skeleton

  • Create the folder tree, the master, 7 sequences and 131 shot assets, at the frame ranges above.
  • Name every shot section exactly as in the sheet, and put a placeholder camera with its default lens in every shot.
  • Add the four black shots, marked frames for each scene start, and dialogue lines as marked frames.

Who: me, fully scripted through the editor connection · Result: a playable 15:00 master to check pacing against

  • Crash site: slope, clearing, wreck, marks, puddles, headlight beams, rain and steam.
  • Ghat road: bend A, the run bends, skid marks and the torn rail. Starts with a check of IceRoad.
  • Hero car and its blockout interior with removable panels. Phones, clock, radio.

Who: me · You review: set layout and wreck orientation, from top-down and marked-up viewport captures

3Cast

Mannequins and pose library

  • Four tinted mannequins with name tags, placed as level actors per set.
  • Control Rig pose library from the list above.

Who: me

4Blocking

Scene-level animation

  • SC04, Pass 1 first. It sets the reset position and the timing of the turn toward the cough.
  • SC05–SC07 next, reusing pass 1's marks. Each pass changes only what the script changes: the head turns early in pass 3, there's no reach for the phone in pass 4, and so on.
  • SC01–SC02: car interior and the stop at the bend.
  • SC03: the run, the skid marks and the gap in the rail.
  • SC08–SC09: the return, mirroring SC01 and SC02.

Who: me, with your notes per scene. Fine performance touch-ups may be quicker by hand in places.

5Cameras

Framing every shot

  • Frame each shot from the sheet's angle and shot-type columns, then build the matched setups by duplication.
  • Moves: lateral track (S03-01), following track (S03-02), push-ins (S05-07, S05-12, S07-25), pans with turns, and the orbit.

Who: me · You review: a contact sheet of one frame per shot

6Review

Renders and editorial handoff

  • Movie Render Queue output per sequence plus the full master, with burn-ins for shot name, frame, lens and caption text.
  • EDL or FCPXML export from the master, so your editor can conform against the shot list.
  • Notes rounds per sequence, starting with the crash site.

Who: me, then you

Proposals for you to approve

Wreck orientation. The script doesn't say which side the car lands on, and the sheet asks for a decision. Vikram kneels at the driver's door in S04-10, so it has to be reachable. My proposal: the car rests on its left (passenger) side, tipped against a tree trunk at about 70°, so the driver's door on the right faces the foot of the slope at kneeling height. Dev ends up low inside the car, Rohan high. The headlights point across the clearing toward the Old Man's mark.

Rain in pass 4. The sheet flags a continuity gap: page 13 says the rain "starts again" in pass 4, but no stop is written before it. My proposal: the rain thins to nothing during the long silence at S07-03, then comes back softly at S07-26 as written. To be confirmed by the director.

Open questions

  1. Frame rate and aspect ratio. Confirmed: 24 fps at 2.39:1.
  2. Real camera and lenses. Matching the virtual filmback and primes to the shoot makes the previz directly usable on set.
  3. Scratch dialogue. Can you record rough reads? Passes 2–4 are 150–225 s of dialogue, and timing them without audio is guesswork.
  4. Run animation. Hand-keyed only, or is a library run cycle acceptable for SQ02?
  5. Environment. Should I try to adapt IceRoad, or go straight to a clean greybox road?
  6. Deadline and team. Is it just you in Unreal, or will others pick up shots? That decides whether shots get split into separate levels for parallel work.
